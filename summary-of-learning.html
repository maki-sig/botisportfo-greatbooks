Synthesis • Tome IV

Summary of Learning

✽

✤ Literature as a Mirror of Human Nature

Taking the Great Books subject made me see how old stories still connect to real life today. Reading through big epics like the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Iliad, and the Odyssey showed me that people back then struggled with the exact same things we do now. Whether it was dealing with too much pride, getting betrayed by people they trusted, or trying to do the right thing, their problems were honestly just like ours.

Because of that, literature stopped feeling like a subject I just had to memorize for school. Looking at Achilles messing things up because of his huge ego, or reading the animal fables in the Panchatantra, made me realize that these stories are actually practical guides for everyday life. They give us simple but solid reminders that staying humble, keeping your patience, and thinking things through will always work out better than just acting on impulse or trying to force your way through.

Critical Methodology

Analytical Growth

✽

✎ Critical Lenses & The CS Mindset

Aside from just following the plot, the subject sharpened my analytical skills by introducing different critical lenses like Structuralism, Feminism, and Archetypes. Using the feminist lens on The Tale of Genji and the Mahabharata gave me concrete tools to look beneath the surface. Instead of just taking things as they are, it helped me spot who really held the power, why characters acted the way they did, and how the overall story was put together.

As a computer science student, breaking down literature with these lenses felt like debugging or tackling a tricky programming problem. Deconstructing stories taught me to step back and look at things from different angles before jumping to conclusions. Overall, it trained me to explain my thoughts more clearly and approach both schoolwork and everyday problems with a much more open, analytical mindset.
