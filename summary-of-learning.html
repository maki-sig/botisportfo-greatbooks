Summary of Learning

✽

✤ Literature as a Mirror of Human Nature

Taking the Great Books course opened my eyes to how ancient and medieval masterpieces connect directly to real human life across eras and cultures. Exploring monumental epics like the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Iliad, and the Odyssey showed me that people back then faced the exact same dilemmas we encounter today—wrestling with excessive pride, navigating betrayal, and honoring sacred duties.

Stories like Achilles' destructive ego and the cautionary animal fables of the Panchatantra proved that literature is not an academic chore meant for memorization, but a living guide to human nature. These timeless narratives provide practical wisdom for our everyday lives, reminding us that humility, patience, and critical discernment always triumph over stubborn impulse and brute force.

Critical Methodology

Analytical Growth

✽

✎ Critical Lenses & The CS Mindset

Beyond plot comprehension, this course sharpened my analytical skills by introducing critical frameworks like the Structuralist, Feminist, and Archetypal lenses. Analyzing The Tale of Genji through feminist lens and the Mahabharata through structural lens gave me the tools to look beneath the surface—uncovering power structures, character motivations, and underlying narrative architectures.

As a computer science student, applying these critical lenses felt remarkably similar to debugging and breaking down complex technical problems. Deconstructing literature trained me to evaluate problems from multiple angles before acting, articulate ideas with greater clarity, and approach both academic challenges and real-world situations with an open, analytical mindset.
