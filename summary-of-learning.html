✤ Literature as a Mirror of Human Nature

Taking the Great Books course really opened my eyes to how stories from different periods and cultures are actually connected to real human life. In our classes, we explored ancient and medieval masterpieces like the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Iliad, the Odyssey, and The Tale of Genji, along with classic fables from the Panchatantra.

At first, I thought these older works would feel totally outdated and hard to relate to, but reading through them showed me that people back then dealt with the exact same struggles we face today, like dealing with pride, holding onto grudges, trusting the wrong people, and trying to honor their duty.

For example, seeing how Achilles let his stubborn ego hurt his own friends, or how the Panchatantra uses simple animal interactions to warn us against blind trust, made me realize that literature works like a mirror of human nature and society. These stories are not just ancient tales to memorize for class, but timeless reminders that teach us practical values like humility, patience, and staying grounded in our everyday lives.

Analytical Growth

✽

✎ Critical Lenses & The CS Mindset

Beyond just understanding the plots, this course helped me build real critical thinking and analytical skills by teaching me how to use different literary lenses. Learning frameworks like the Structuralist, Archetypal, Feminist, Psychoanalytic, and Ecocritical lenses gave me specific tools to look beneath the surface of a text instead of just taking things at face value.

For instance, when I analyzed The Tale of Genji through a feminist lens or the Mahabharata through structuralism, it forced me to ask deeper questions about who holds power, how character relationships create conflict, and why authors choose specific narrative patterns.

As a computer science student, breaking down stories this way felt a lot like analyzing a complex problem from multiple angles before jumping to conclusions. Applying these critical lenses trained me to think more carefully, express my ideas more clearly, and evaluate both what I read and the real-world situations I encounter with a much more open and analytical mindset.
