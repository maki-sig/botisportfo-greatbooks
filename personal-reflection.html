Evolving Appreciation

✽
Core Inquiry I

How has your understanding and appreciation of literature changed throughout the course?

✤ Personal Realization & Insights

Coming from a computer science background, I used to think literature was just long, boring stories detached from practical life. But going through this course showed me that these works actually mirror real human experiences. Stories like the Greek epics and Panchatantra fables deal with the exact same issues we face today, such as pride, trust, and duty. Literature stopped feeling like a chore and became a relatable guide to understanding how people handle life's challenges.

Chapter II • Tome V

Overcoming Challenges

✽
Core Inquiry II

What challenges did you encounter when reading and analyzing literary masterpieces, and how did you overcome them?

✎ Methodological Breakthrough

My biggest hurdle was getting past the archaic language, massive casts, and complex family trees in epics like the Mahabharata and the Iliad. It was easy to get lost in the details. I overcame this by treating stories like programming problems, breaking them down step by step. I focused on core conflicts, summarized key events in simple words, and connected the themes to storytelling I already enjoy, like character-driven manga, which made the texts much easier to digest.

Chapter III • Tome V

The Structuralist Lens

✽
Core Inquiry III

Which literary lens or approach influenced your interpretation of literature the most? Why?

✶ Systems & Relational Structures

The Structuralist lens influenced me the most because I naturally look for patterns, logic, and systems. Instead of getting caught up in raw emotions, it helped me see how a story's building blocks, opposite pairs, and relationships work together. In Book 2 of the Panchatantra, for instance, the crow, mouse, deer, and turtle function like a working machine where each animal's distinct trait is necessary for the team to survive. Viewing texts as organized systems made analyzing them straightforward and clear.

Chapter IV • Tome V

Human Nature & Choices

✽
Core Inquiry IV

What important realizations about life, society, culture, or human nature have you gained?

✤ Pride, Humility & Consequence

I realized that no matter the era or culture, basic human flaws never change: unchecked pride causes disaster, blind trust brings betrayal, and grudges hurt everyone. Achilles and Roland both showed how stubborn ego leads to tragic losses, while the Odyssey and Panchatantra proved that patience and quick wits beat brute force every time. It reminded me that life is fragile, and we need to stay humble, thoughtful, and accountable for our actions.

Practical Application

✽
Core Inquiry V

How can you apply the lessons and values learned from Great Books in your personal, academic, or future professional life?

✎ Engineering, Teamwork & Ethics

In my personal life, these stories remind me to be a loyal friend while staying mindful of who I trust. Academically and in my tech career, analyzing literature strengthens my problem-solving skills by training me to look at issues from multiple angles before acting. When working on group projects or building software, the lesson of the Panchatantra allies reminds me to value each teammate's unique strengths so we can succeed together. Most of all, it taught me to stay grounded and lead with patience and empathy.
